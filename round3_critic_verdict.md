# Round 3 — The Critic's Final Verdict

## Direct answer: does the 4-event Indigenous spine pass G2?

**Yes — conditionally.** I'm updating from my round-1 position. Socrates' distinction is correct and I over-applied the gate: G2 blocks *branch content that pretends to model systems the engine doesn't have* (consent processes, nation-to-nation negotiation, First Nations as an electoral region). It does not block *federal-level decision points*, which is what all four of these are. Each spine event is a Cabinet/federal decision with two historically real options — exactly the shape of every other universal anchor already shipping.

**The concrete counterexample test (White Paper 1969):** I tried to construct the failure mode and couldn't — *provided* one authoring constraint holds. The event is honest as metrics+flag because the real 1969 decision was a federal policy choice (pursue the White Paper vs. withdraw after Indigenous political response); no consent mechanic existed or is being simulated. The failure mode exists only if we smuggle deferred mechanics back in through copy — e.g., a "negotiate with nations" third option, or approval deltas implying modeled Indigenous constituencies. That's the trap, so:

**Authoring constraints on the spine (binding):**
1. Choices must be the historically real federal options only — no invented "consultation" or "negotiate" branches.
2. Effects stay on national metrics (`rights`, `unity`, `approval`, `sovereignty`) — no regional deltas, since regional support doesn't exist yet.
3. Flags are contractual: `indigenousRelation`, `s35Recognized`, `treatyPosture`, `reconciliationPath` are the documented precondition set for the future arc and must not be repurposed.
4. If any of the four events can't be written honestly under 1–2, it drops rather than bends — 4 is the target, 3 acceptable.
